About This Chart

The Annual Temperature Profile shows the 30-year average daily high and low temperatures for every day of the year at your location. These are not forecasts — they represent the long-term expected temperature envelope for a typical year.

Data source: NCEI (National Centers for Environmental Information) daily station observations — TMAX and TMIN from the nearest first-order ASOS/airport weather station. For each calendar day (January 1 through December 31), daily maximum and minimum temperatures are averaged across the most recent 30-year period available from NCEI station records. If NCEI station data is unavailable or returns insufficient coverage, an error is shown and no estimated data is substituted.

The green dashed vertical line marks today's date on the chart, so you can quickly see how current conditions compare to the seasonal average. Temperatures are displayed in °F.

About These Records

The Record High and Record Low values show the most extreme temperatures ever recorded for today's calendar date at your location, based on 30 years of daily NCEI station observations.

Data source: NCEI (National Centers for Environmental Information) daily station data — TMAX and TMIN from the nearest first-order ASOS/airport weather station. For today's calendar date (month and day), all available station records are scanned and the single highest TMAX and lowest TMIN are reported as the all-time records for that date. The year shown is the year in which that extreme was recorded. If NCEI station data is unavailable or contains insufficient records for this location, an error will be shown in place of the record values.

Delta vs. today's forecast compares the record value to today's forecast high or low from the Open-Meteo 7-day model. A positive delta means today's forecast is below the record; a negative delta means today's forecast meets or exceeds the historical record.

About Extreme Temperature Counts

These figures show the average number of extreme temperature days per year at the nearest NCEI weather station, based on 30 years of daily observations.

  • #Days with Highs ≥ 90°F — counts each calendar day in a year where the recorded daily maximum temperature (TMAX) reached or exceeded 90°F. These are classified as "hot days" and are relevant for heat stress on turf, irrigation demand, and summer dormancy risk.
  • #Days with Lows ≤ 32°F / yr — counts each calendar day in a year where the recorded daily minimum temperature (TMIN) was at or below 32°F (freezing). These affect frost and freeze risk, dormancy entry and exit, and overseeding windows.

Data source: NCEI (National Centers for Environmental Information) daily station summaries (GHCND) from the nearest first-order ASOS/airport weather station, fetched via the NCEI Climate Data Online API. The average shown represents the mean annual count across the 30-year period. The year-by-year table shows the raw count for each individual year.

If NCEI station data is unavailable for your location, an error will be displayed and no estimated values will be substituted.

About the Frost & Freeze Window

These dates represent the statistically likely last spring freeze and first fall freeze for your location. A "freeze" is defined as a day when the minimum temperature falls to 32°F (0°C) or below.

Data source: NCEI (National Centers for Environmental Information) daily station observations — TMIN from the nearest first-order ASOS/airport weather station, covering up to 30 years of records. For each calendar date, the fraction of years on which a freeze was recorded is computed to derive probability thresholds.

Probability thresholds: Two dates are shown for each event:

  • Typical (50%) — The date by which there is a 50% historical probability that the last (or first) freeze has occurred. This is the median freeze date across the record period.
  • Possible (10%) — The more conservative threshold. For last spring freeze, this is the date by which only 10% of years had their last freeze — meaning 90% of years still experienced a freeze after this date. For first fall freeze, it means 90% of years had their first freeze before this date. Use these dates for frost-sensitive plantings.

Frost-Free Growing Window is the number of days between the typical last spring freeze (50%) and the typical first fall freeze (50%). This represents the expected frost-free growing season length for your area.

About Solar & Lunar Data

Sunrise, Sunset & Total Daylight are sourced from the Open-Meteo Forecast API, which provides precise sunrise and sunset times for your exact latitude and longitude. Daylight duration is computed as the difference between sunset and sunrise. The trend indicator (↑ / ↓) compares today's daylight duration to yesterday's.

Lunar Phase is calculated directly in the browser using the Julian Day Number algorithm. The current phase position is derived from the number of days elapsed since a known New Moon epoch (January 6, 2000), divided by the mean synodic period of 29.53 days. This calculation requires no external API call and is accurate to within approximately ±1 day for phase name and illumination estimate.

Daylight Arc is a visual representation of the sun's position across the sky for today. The arc shows the proportion of the 24-hour day that falls between sunrise and sunset, with the sun position dot showing the approximate current time of day relative to the full daylight window.

About the Daily Averages Cards

Each card represents one of the past five complete days (not including today) and displays three key soil and atmosphere measurements derived from Open-Meteo's hourly ERA5-based archive data.

  • ET₀ (Evapotranspiration) — Daily reference evapotranspiration in inches, calculated using the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith method from hourly temperature, humidity, wind speed, and solar radiation data. ET₀ represents the atmospheric evaporative demand for a standardized well-watered grass surface.
  • Soil Temp — Daily average soil temperature at 6 cm depth in °F. This shallow-layer temperature closely tracks surface conditions and is highly relevant for seed germination timing, fungal activity thresholds, and root zone management.
  • GDD (Growing Degree Days) — Daily GDD accumulation using the standard formula: GDD = max(0, ((Tmax + Tmin) / 2) − 50°F), where Tmax and Tmin are the daily maximum and minimum soil temperatures at 6 cm depth. A base temperature of 50°F is used, which aligns with the threshold below which most cool-season turfgrass metabolic activity is minimal.

Data is sourced from the Open-Meteo ERA5 archive API with hourly resolution, aggregated to daily values. Soil measurements are at the 6 cm depth layer.

About the Growing Degree Day Values

Growing Degree Days (GDD) quantify the thermal energy available for plant and pest development on a given day. Rather than counting calendar days, GDD accounts for the fact that biological processes accelerate with warmth and slow in cool conditions.

Formula: GDD = max(0, ((Tmax + Tmin) / 2) − Tbase)

A base temperature of 50°F is used throughout this dashboard. Days where the average temperature is at or below 50°F contribute zero GDD. Soil temperature at 6 cm depth is used as the thermal input (rather than air temperature) because it better represents the rhizosphere environment where turfgrass roots, pathogens, and soil organisms are active.

  • Today's High Air Temp — The forecast or observed daily maximum air temperature at 2m height for today.
  • Soil Temp (6 cm) — The current soil temperature reading at 6 cm depth from the Open-Meteo forecast model.
  • Today's GDD — GDD accumulated for the current day using soil temperature Tmax and Tmin at 6 cm, base 50°F.
  • Cumulative tGDD (YTD) — The sum of all daily GDD values from January 1 through today. This running total is used for tracking phenological milestones such as crabgrass germination, fungicide timing windows, and overseeding schedules.

Historical GDD is sourced from the Open-Meteo ERA5-Land archive; today's and forecast GDD uses the Open-Meteo 7-day forecast model.

About the Cumulative GDD Chart

This chart visualizes the full-year thermal accumulation from January 1 through today, combining two complementary views of GDD data.

  • Daily GDD bars (gold, left axis) — The GDD accumulated on each individual day, calculated as max(0, ((Tmax + Tmin) / 2) − 50°F) using soil temperature at 6 cm depth with a base of 50°F. Taller bars indicate warmer days with more thermal energy available.
  • Cumulative tGDD line (green, right axis) — The running year-to-date total of GDD, showing the overall thermal progression through the season. The steepness of the curve reflects the intensity of the growing season: a steep climb indicates rapid heat accumulation; a flat section means cool temperatures with little biological activity.

Data sources: Historical daily values (January 1 through approximately 5 days ago) are derived from the Open-Meteo ERA5-Land reanalysis archive. Recent and current-day values use the Open-Meteo 7-day forecast model. All GDD values use soil temperature at 6 cm depth and a base temperature of 50°F.

Common turfgrass benchmarks tracked with cumulative GDD include: crabgrass germination (typically 200–300 tGDD), annual bluegrass seedhead emergence, and fungicide reapplication timing for dollar spot and brown patch prevention.

About the PACE Turf Growth Potential Model

The PACE Turf Growth Potential (GP) model, developed by Dr. Larry Stowell and Dr. Wendy Gelernter, estimates the relative rate of turfgrass growth on a 0–100% scale based on soil temperature, using a Gaussian equation that peaks at the species' optimal temperature.

Formula: GP = exp(−0.5 × ((T − Topt) / σ)²) × 100, where T is daily average soil temperature at 6 cm depth (°F). Today's value uses the current hourly reading; the 3-day forecast strip uses daily averages. Result is clamped 0–100 and rounded to the nearest integer.

  • T — Soil temperature at 6 cm depth (°F) from Open-Meteo forecast.
  • Topt — Optimal temperature for cool-season turf: 68°F (20°C). GP = 100% at this temperature.
  • σ — Spread parameter: 10°F. GP drops to ~61% at ±10°F from optimal, ~14% at ±20°F.

Growth Zones:

  • GP ≥ 70% (High) — Active growth; aggressive programs appropriate.
  • GP 40–70% (Moderate) — Reduced growth; moderate inputs.
  • GP 20–40% (Low) — Slow recovery; minimize stress.
  • GP < 20% (Minimal) — Near dormancy or stress; avoid inputs.

Model applies to cool-season grasses (tall fescue, KBG, ryegrass, fine fescue). Warm-season grasses have Topt ≈ 85°F and are not modeled here. Data source: Open-Meteo Forecast API · soil temperature 6 cm depth.

How This Is Calculated

Today: Today's precipitation total is sourced from NWS hourly ASOS station observations (the same source weather.gov uses). The value is the sum of all precipitationLastHour readings since local midnight at the nearest NWS station. This updates in near real-time as new hourly observations are posted — typically within 1 hour of actual precipitation. If no hourly data is available, the card shows "—".

This Month & Year-to-Date: Monthly and YTD totals are accumulated directly from the nearest NCEI GHCN-Daily station (typically within 50 km). Values use the units=standard endpoint, which returns precipitation in decimal inches. No model or reanalysis data is used.

Departure from Normal: The departure compares the Year-to-Date observed total against the 30-year climatological normal for the same Jan 1 – present window, computed from the same NCEI station's historical record (prior 30 years). How This Is Calculated (click to expand)

This table estimates weekly turfgrass irrigation need using the FAO-56 crop coefficient method: Irrigation Need (in/week) = ET₀ × Kc × 7, where ET₀ is the reference evapotranspiration and Kc is a dimensionless crop coefficient specific to each grass type and stress condition.

ET₀ (Reference Evapotranspiration) represents the evaporative demand of the atmosphere — how much water a well-watered reference grass surface would lose per day. It is calculated using the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation, which accounts for temperature, solar radiation, humidity, and wind speed. Values are sourced from Open-Meteo's hourly forecast data and averaged over the next 7 days.

Kc (Crop Coefficient) adjusts ET₀ for the specific water-use characteristics of each turfgrass species and management condition:

  • Normal Conditions — Active growth, adequate soil moisture, no significant heat stress. Grass is transpiring at near-potential rates. Use these values for routine irrigation scheduling during the growing season.
  • Deficit Conditions — Heat stress, limited soil moisture, or intentional water restriction. Reduced Kc reflects partial stomatal closure and lower transpiration. Grass may show mild wilting but recovers with irrigation. Use these values when implementing drought management or water conservation programs.
  • Dormancy Conditions — Grass has entered summer or winter dormancy and metabolic activity is minimal. Irrigation at this stage is intended only to keep crowns and root systems alive, not to maintain green color or active growth. Use the lowest values when managing dormant turf through extended drought or heat events.

Ranges reflect the natural variation in published Kc values across research studies, soil types, and climate regions. Subtract recent rainfall from the calculated weekly need before irrigating. Actual application rates should also account for irrigation system efficiency, slope, and soil infiltration rate.

How This Is Calculated
The Smith-Kerns Dollar Spot Model

The Dollar Spot Vulnerability index is calculated using the Smith-Kerns model (Smith & Kerns, 2018 · PLOS ONE), a logistic regression model that predicts the probability of dollar spot infection based on recent temperature and relative humidity conditions.

Step 1 — Daily Disease Severity Value (DSV): For each hour in the rolling 7-day window, a disease severity contribution is calculated as:

  • If the air temperature is between 15°C (59°F) and 35°C (95°F), and the relative humidity is ≥ 80%, the hourly contribution is: T_C × (RH / 100), where T_C is the temperature in Celsius.
  • Hours outside these temperature or humidity thresholds contribute 0.
  • All hourly contributions for a calendar day are summed to produce the daily DSV.

Step 2 — 7-Day Average DSV: The daily DSV values for the past 7 days (including today) are averaged to produce the 7-day rolling DSV average.

Step 3 — Logistic Probability: The 7-day DSV average is converted to an infection probability using the logistic equation: P = eX / (1 + eX), where X = −19.65 + 0.202 × DSV_avg. The result is expressed as a percentage (0–100%).

Risk Tiers: <25% = Low Risk · 25–49% = Moderate · 50–74% = High Risk · ≥75% = Severe. Fungicide application thresholds should be adjusted based on turf value, local disease history, and product mode of action.

Data source: Hourly air temperature and relative humidity from the Open-Meteo Forecast API (past 5 days + today), aggregated to daily DSV values.

Fungicide Residual Model

This model estimates the remaining efficacy of a fungicide application over time using Growing Degree Days (GDD) accumulated since the application date. Rather than counting calendar days, GDD accounts for temperature-driven biological activity — warmer conditions accelerate fungal growth and fungicide breakdown, while cooler conditions slow both.

GDD Accumulation is calculated daily using the formula: GDD = max(0, ((Tmax + Tmin) / 2) − Tbase), where Tbase is 50°F. Soil temperature at 6 cm depth is used when available, as it better reflects the rhizosphere environment where turf pathogens are active. Historical GDD is sourced from the Open-Meteo ERA5 archive; forecast GDD uses the 7-day Penman-Monteith forecast.

Residual Zones are defined as:

  • Strong Protection — GDD accumulated since application is below the typical threshold at which fungicide efficacy begins to decline significantly. Full preventive protection is expected.
  • Declining Protection — GDD has reached the range where efficacy is diminishing. Turf remains protected but re-application should be planned, especially if high disease pressure conditions (warm nights, leaf wetness) are present.
  • Re-application Recommended — GDD has exceeded the general residual window for most common turfgrass fungicides. Protection is likely insufficient, and re-application is advisable to maintain preventive coverage.

Residual windows vary by active ingredient, application rate, and disease target. Always consult the product label for specific re-application intervals. This model is a general guide and does not replace label directions or agronomic judgment.
