How This Is Calculated

This table estimates weekly turfgrass irrigation need using the FAO-56 crop coefficient method: Irrigation Need (in/week) = ET₀ × Kc × 7, where ET₀ is the reference evapotranspiration and Kc is a dimensionless crop coefficient specific to each grass type and stress condition.

ET₀ (Reference Evapotranspiration) represents the evaporative demand of the atmosphere — how much water a well-watered reference grass surface would lose per day. It is calculated using the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith equation, which accounts for temperature, solar radiation, humidity, and wind speed. Values are sourced from Open-Meteo's hourly forecast data and averaged over the next 7 days.

Kc (Crop Coefficient) adjusts ET₀ for the specific water-use characteristics of each turfgrass species and management condition:

  • Normal Conditions — Active growth, adequate soil moisture, no significant heat stress. Grass is transpiring at near-potential rates. Use these values for routine irrigation scheduling during the growing season.
  • Drought Conditions — Heat stress, limited soil moisture, or intentional water restriction. Reduced Kc reflects partial stomatal closure and lower transpiration. Grass may show mild wilting but recovers with irrigation. Use these values when implementing drought management or water conservation programs.
  • Dormancy Conditions — Grass has entered summer or winter dormancy and metabolic activity is minimal. Irrigation at this stage is intended only to keep crowns and root systems alive, not to maintain green color or active growth. Use the lowest values when managing dormant turf through extended drought or heat events.

Ranges reflect the natural variation in published Kc values across research studies, soil types, and climate regions. Subtract recent rainfall from the calculated weekly need before irrigating. Actual application rates should also account for irrigation system efficiency, slope, and soil infiltration rate.
